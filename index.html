Developmental cognitive neuroscience

How brains grow, and what shapes the way they do.

NeuroRoots Lab studies how the mind and the brain change during the first years of life, both for the infants and their parents, becasue we know that when a baby is born, a parent is also born.

Explore our research Meet the team
Research

Three lines of research

Our work connects the brain to behaviour across early development, from the first months of life through childhood.

01

Development of brain dynamics

How do coordinated patterns of brain activity emerge, stabilise and change as children grow, and what do they tell us about later development?

Participate!
02

Neurodivergence in development

How does neurodivergent development unfold across brain, behaviour and cognition, and how can we understand differences as part of the diversity of development?

Participate!
03

Post-partum depression (MAP-PD)

What are the brain and behavioural signs of early post-partum depression emergence, and how do they relate to emotion, memory and parenting?

Participate!
Methods

An interdisciplinary toolkit

No single measure captures a developing brain. We combine neuroimaging with behaviour and cognition to see the whole picture.

EEG

Millisecond-level recordings of the brain's electrical activity.

fNIRS

Light-based imaging of cortical activity, well suited to infants and young children.

fMRI

Whole-brain imaging of activity and connectivity.

Eye tracking

Where and how long we look: a window on attention and early cognition.

Behavioural observation

Structured and naturalistic observation of behaviour and interaction.

Cognitive testing

Standardised measures of attention, memory and executive function.

Take part in our research

PaTIE study: Understanding parent-infant interactions during play

We are studying how parents and young children interact during play. Families can take part if their child is between 18 months and 4 years old. The study is based in our lab at Queen's University Belfast (QUB).

To take part, email a.truzzi[at]qub.ac.uk.

Neurodivergence in development

There is currently no ongoing data collection for this area of research.

Memories and post-partum depression study (MAP-PD)

This study investigates how memories of your own infancy relate to post-partum depression. You can take part if you are between 0 and 4 months post-partum. Participation involves an initial online questionnaire with our researchers, followed by one lab visit about 2-3 months later. During the visit, we measure brain responses while you complete a cognitive task.

To take part, email sclerkin05@qub.ac.uk.
